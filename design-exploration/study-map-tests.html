Test 3 covers what came after Test 2: Weeks 9–13. The map draws each class's units, so picking a test shows its stretch, and ticking items off fills it in mint.

The data is the app's sample classes spread across a fall semester. Week 1 starts Mon, Aug 24, and today is Sat, Nov 21 (Week 13). Every preview is clickable and has its own light/dark switch.

A. Clock Recommended

The semester goes once around the class, with a mint hand pointing at today.

The ring hugging the class is split into units, one per test. Picking a test sweeps its unit in and shades its weeks. Weeks always sit in the same place, so Week 9 is always at about seven o'clock.

  • Good: exactly your class → weeks → assignments idea, and the hand shows how far through the term you are.
  • Good: the unit ring shows how the whole semester is split before you pick anything.
  • Trade-off: one class at a time, and not draggable.

B. Springy tree

The same structure, with the map's current floaty, draggable feel.

Weeks are hubs joined in order, so they curl around the class like a trail. The picked unit becomes a soft region drawn around its weeks, and it follows them when you drag.

  • Good: keeps what the map has now (dragging, the organic look).
  • Trade-off: the shape shifts a little each time, so finding Week 9 takes a second look.
  • Trade-off: one class at a time.

C. Subway lines

Every class is a line, weeks are stops, and tests are stations.

All classes run on one week scale with a mint "today" line. Picking a test thickens that stretch of track and names every stop on it. Reviewing draws a mint line along it.

  • Good: all classes at once, so you see Math Test 3 and the CS final land close together.
  • Trade-off: a sideways timeline more than a map. It drops the class-in-the-center idea.
  • Trade-off: gets tall with 5–6 classes.
praedus · Study map

Tests are found by name: "test", "exam" or "midterm". A bare "final" doesn't count, so "Essay 1 final" isn't a test, but "Final exam" is. A final exam covers the whole semester.

Reviewed ticks are shared across the three previews, so you can compare how each one shows progress.
