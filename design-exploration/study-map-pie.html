One class, one chart

Pick a class from the dropdown. The chart is its semester: the class in the center, split into weeks. The panel lists the class's upcoming tests; pick one and the chart highlights the weeks it covers. Then Make a study set pulls the topics from those weeks so an AI can turn them into a study set (marked as coming with Premium).

Same demo semester (today is Week 13). Everything is clickable, with a light/dark switch on each preview.

A. Donut Recommended

Sixteen equal slices, one per week. Small dots in each slice are that week's work.

The covered weeks fill with the class color; everything else stays a quiet gray. Tests sit on the rim where they fall, and the current week has a mint outline.

  • Good: the cleanest, most "pie chart" of the four. Readable at a glance.
  • Good: weeks are always in the same spot, so Week 9 is always at about seven o'clock.
  • Trade-off: assignment names live in the panel (and on hover), not on the chart.

B. Sunburst

Weeks on the inner ring; every assignment gets its own slice on the outer ring.

The outer ring shows status: done work is gray, unfinished work is the class color, missing work is red, and anything you've reviewed turns mint.

  • Good: shows the most at once, and progress is easy to see.
  • Trade-off: busier than A; small slices for busy weeks.
  • Trade-off: names still need a hover or the panel.
praedus · Study map

C. Workload pie

Each week's slice reaches out as far as that week's workload.

Uses the time estimates the app already has, so heavy weeks (a project, a test) stick out and light weeks stay small. Covered weeks fill with the class color.

  • Good: answers "how much was in this unit?" without reading anything.
  • Trade-off: less symmetrical than A, so a little less calm.
  • Trade-off: depends on estimates being roughly right.

D. Semester track My own idea

The semester as sixteen columns, left to right, with every assignment written out.

Tests are flags above their week, the covered weeks get a shaded band, and a mint line marks today. Not a pie, but every name is readable without hovering, which is what makes it feel professional to me.

  • Good: the most readable: you can scan a whole unit's work in one look.
  • Good: fits phones better (it can scroll sideways).
  • Trade-off: drops the class-in-the-center idea.
